# Gene-Co-expression-Prediction
## Abstract

This study investigates the development and evaluation of a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model for predicting gene expression correlation patterns in biological data. We assessed the model under two scenarios: 1) training on randomly ordered gene pairs, and 2) training and testing on gene sets with no overlap. Results demonstrate robust performance on training data, achieving Mean Squared Error (MSE) of 0.00183 and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.02846 in the first scenario, and MSE of 0.00142 and MAE of 0.02638 in the second. The model also generalizes well in the first scenario, with test set MSE of 0.00321 and MAE of 0.03448, reflecting effective prediction of gene correlations (ranging from -1 to 1). However, in the second scenario, the model overfits, yielding test MSE of 0.17506 and MAE of 0.33843, indicating it memorizes training patterns rather than learning generalizable features. This highlights the critical role of gene diversity in training data and suggests leveraging regularization or weighting techniques to enhance model generalizability for practical applications in genomics and bioinformatics.
